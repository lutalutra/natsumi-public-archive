Field Notes of Topophilia

2017 · Research and writing研究・論考

How does a person come to recognize a place as part of oneself? Inspired by humanistic geography, an essay that takes apart places, environments and their memories, based on field research in Weimar, Germany. Its three parts, Thoughts, Conversations and Quotes, were printed on tracing paper as a booklet and shown as an installation. The master’s thesis project of the MFA programme Public Art and New Artistic Strategies, Bauhaus-Universität Weimar, presented at the Herzogin Anna Amalia Bibliothek.人は、どのように場所を自分の一部として認識するのか。人文主義地理学に触発され、ドイツ・ワイマールでのフィールドリサーチをもとに、場所と環境の記憶を分解しながら考えたエッセイ。「Thoughts」「Conversations」「Quotes」の3つの部分を、トレーシングペーパーに印刷した冊子とインスタレーションにまとめた。Bauhaus-Universität Weimar の修士課程「Public Art and New Artistic Strategies」の修了研究として、ゲーテが監督を務めた時代からの蔵書を収める Herzogin Anna Amalia Bibliothek で発表。

Notation of Topophilia, Field Notes

Abstract概要（論文の冒頭より、英語）

This research poses questions about the correlations between places and human emotions. How do human beings recognise a place as a part of one’s self? My aim is to take note of the emotional relationships between humans and places. While experimenting with a variety of approaches, I found research from a variety of fields of study. I am influenced by the fields of design and humanistic geography, which leads me to repeatedly return to the question “what is place?” Inspired by conversations with people, I aim to show new perspectives about place through various forms.

This research begins with human consciousness, which is comprised of internal worlds. However, these internal worlds are made up of our surrounding environments. Environments create humans, humans create environments. When taking into account people’s memories, the correlation between memory and topographical space becomes clear.

Consider the question: “Would you tell me about your place?” Potential answers to this question may produce stories about a region or the informant herself. It is impossible to define a clear border between the two. The ultimate aim of this research is to get to know places as mirrored by humans, as well as to get to know humans as mirrored by places. My research crosses disciplines such as environmental design and public art, and is influenced by my life in both Japan and Germany. The process of making artwork is utilised for visualising notions of place which are usually hidden in the inner worlds of individuals. The works are generated through observations and collected interviews in several target cities, and will be further explained in this text.
